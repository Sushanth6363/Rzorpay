## The 60-second answer, if you read nothing else

**What this is:** Razorpay is hiring interns by asking students to build something, not by reading
CVs. ₹75,000/month, Bangalore, 6 or 12 months. You pick one of five tracks, build a project, submit
a GitHub repo and a 5-minute video. If it shows promise, you go straight to a panel interview.

**What they actually want:** Not a clever idea. **Evidence.** Every track says some version of the
same three things — *run it over a batch of records, publish the number, and be honest about what
it got wrong.* Most applicants will demo one success and stop. That's the whole gap.

**What we found:** We researched Razorpay's real business problems, found 20, and narrowed them to
five projects worth building. Along the way we killed nine ideas because **Razorpay already sells
them** — including nine of the examples listed on their own website.

**Our recommendation:** Build the **reconciliation exception resolver** (Track 4). It's the safest
strong choice: the data is easy to get, the track is the least crowded, and there's a genuinely
interesting reason nobody has built it yet.

**The one thing that could change this:** We couldn't check Razorpay's current product list — their
page wouldn't load properly for us. Our information is five months old. Two of the five options
depend on that being accurate.

## A note on honesty

You'll notice these files admit mistakes. We got a regulation wrong and had to reverse it. We
counted something as 24 when it was 22. We called two ideas "novel" and later found companies
already selling them.

That's deliberate. **You're going to defend this work in front of a Razorpay engineer who knows
this domain far better than we do.** Knowing which parts are rock-solid and which are shaky is
worth more than a confident story that falls apart under one good question.
