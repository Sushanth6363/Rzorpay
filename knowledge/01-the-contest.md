# 1. The Contest — what you're actually entering

## It looks like a hackathon. It isn't.

There's no prize money. No leaderboard. No "top 3 teams win."

What's on offer is a **job**: a 6 or 12 month AI Builder Internship at Razorpay, ₹75,000 a month,
in person in Bangalore. You build something, submit it, and if it shows promise you're pulled
straight into a panel interview.

That single fact changes everything about how you should build.

| A hackathon rewards | This rewards |
|---|---|
| The best three projects | Everyone who clears a bar |
| A demo that makes people go "wow" | Evidence you can be trusted near real money |
| A team's combined output | **Yours alone** — the form has no space for teammates |
| Being judged once, then forgotten | Being judged, then **cross-examined live** |

Read that last row again. Your repo isn't the deliverable. **Your repo is the evidence in a job
interview.** Somebody who works on payments every day is going to open it and ask you questions.

Anything in there you can't explain line by line is working against you.

---

## The four steps, in Razorpay's own words

> *"Pick a track, build something real, show your work (a public repo, a 5 minute pitch video, the
> architecture), and if it has signal we call you in."*

And on how they screen: *"No resume screening. No long application."* Then later:
*"Shortlisted builders go straight to a panel. No aptitude test. No group discussion."*

So: no CV filter, no puzzles. Just the thing you built, and then a conversation about it.

---

## The five tracks

You pick exactly one.

1. **AI Growth & Agentic Commerce** — help a shop earn more, or let an AI shopper buy from it
2. **AI Risk Manager** — stop a shop losing money to fraud, returns and disputes
3. **AI Revenue Recovery** — find money slipping away and win it back
4. **AI Finance Controller** — run the books and the cash position
5. **Open Track** — build what you think should exist

---

## The thing almost everyone will miss

Under each track Razorpay wrote a line called **"The bar."** These lines are the actual grading
criteria, and they are strikingly consistent. Stripped of the packaging, all five say the same
three things:

> **1. Run it over a batch — not one example.**
> Track 4 literally says *"a 50+ record batch."* Track 2 says *"a held-out test set."*
> Track 4 also says: *"One cherry-picked match proves nothing."*
>
> **2. Publish a real number.**
> Money recovered. Match rate. Precision and recall. An actual measured figure.
>
> **3. Be honest about what it got wrong.**
> Track 4 wants *"the exceptions it could not resolve."* Track 2 wants *"honest metrics including
> false-positive cost."* Track 1 wants *"one failure handled gracefully."*

That third one is the strange one, and it's the most important. **Razorpay is explicitly asking you
to show them your failures.**

Most people will build a demo where everything works, record it, and submit. They'll be answering
a question nobody asked.

## What we saw in the competition

We looked at public GitHub repos tagged for this buildathon. We found **300**, and **293 of them
were created in a five-day window**. It's a stampede, and it peaked while we were looking.

Then we did something more useful. We took eight repos whose descriptions used the strongest
language — "bounded", "auditable", "explainable", the exact vocabulary from Razorpay's bars — and
opened their READMEs to see whether the work matched the words.

**One out of eight** had actual evaluation evidence: real metrics, a held-out test set, a baseline,
a failure analysis. Three were partial. Four were nearly empty.

So the vocabulary is already everywhere — "bounded" appears in 36 repo descriptions, "audit" in 35.
**Saying it is worth nothing now. Only showing it counts.**

That's genuinely good news for you. The bar is high but the field is mostly not clearing it.

## Practical things that will bite you

**One shot, no edits.** The submission form has a checkbox: *"I confirm that this is my official
final project submission. I understand that no further changes or edits can be made after
submitting."* So freeze the repo, tag it, and make sure your video matches the code exactly. A repo
that's moved on from its own video looks careless.

**Five minutes means five minutes.** Budget it. Most of it should be the thing running.

**You must be a student graduating in 2027, 2028 or 2029**, and available in Bangalore in person
from September. Both are dropdowns on the form — hard gates, not preferences.

**The deadline is probably 5 September 2026** — but Razorpay has *never published it*. It's not on
their page, not in the form, not in the page's hidden event data. It appears only in third-party
blogs. Plan for it; don't quote it as fact.
