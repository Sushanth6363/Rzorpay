# growth_history.md — Razorpay's Growth, 2014–2026

Compiled 2026-08-25. Grading: **[A]** official Razorpay · **[B]** primary external ·
**[C]** credible secondary · **[E]** derived here.

⚠️ **Most of this is [C].** Razorpay is private and pre-IPO; its DRHP was filed confidentially, so
there is **no audited public financial history**. Figures come from regulatory filings reported by
trade press (Entrackr, Inc42, Business Standard). Where sources conflict, both are shown.

## 1. Financial growth

### Revenue from operations

| Year | Revenue (₹ cr) | Growth |
|---|---|---|
| FY21 | 841 | — |
| FY22 | 1,481 | **+76.1%** |
| FY23 | 2,279 | **+53.9%** |
| **FY24** | **2,296** | **+0.7%** ⚠️ |
| FY25 | **3,783** | **+64.8%** |

**FY21→FY25 CAGR: 45.6%**

⚠️ **A conflicting FY24 figure of ₹2,475 cr also circulates** — likely total income versus revenue
from operations, or a restatement. The widely-quoted "+65% in FY25" uses the ₹2,296 cr base. Say
which you're using.

### ⭐ The flat year, and what caused it

**FY23 → FY24 growth was 0.7%.** Revenue essentially stopped.

The timing is not a coincidence. **The RBI barred Razorpay from onboarding new merchants from around
December 2022 until December 2023** — pending its final Payment Aggregator licence. That window maps
almost exactly onto FY24.

A regulator switched off new-customer acquisition for roughly a year, and the revenue line went flat
for exactly that year. **[B/E]** It is the single clearest illustration in this company's history of
how much regulatory standing matters to a payments business — and it recovered to +65% the year
after the licence came through.

### Profitability

| Year | Net result (₹ cr) | As % of revenue |
|---|---|---|
| FY22 | +7.3 | 0.5% |
| FY23 | +7.2 | 0.3% |
| FY24 | +34 | 1.5% |
| **FY25** | **−1,209** | **−32%** |

**Profit was always thin — never above 1.5% of revenue.** The FY25 loss is not an operating collapse:
it is ESOP charges plus roughly ₹1,245 cr (~$150M) of tax from moving the parent company from the US
to India. Stripping the one-offs, **gross profit grew 41% to ₹1,277 cr** and online payments was
EBITDA-positive.

### Payment volume

| Year | TPV | Growth |
|---|---|---|
| FY24 | $150bn | — |
| FY25 | **$180bn** | **+20%** |
| 2030 target | **$400bn** | 17.3% CAGR required |

**Revenue grew ~3× faster than volume in FY25** (+65% vs +20%). Implied take rate rose from **0.199%
to 0.253%, +27% relative** **[E]**. Growth is coming from monetising each merchant more deeply, not
from moving more money — the rational response to a dominant rail that earns zero MDR.

⚠️ **Two different 2030 targets circulate**: ~$400bn TPV, and a CFO statement about **$750bn P2M by
2030**. The second is likely an ecosystem or P2M-market figure rather than Razorpay's own volume.
Don't conflate them.

## 7. The arc, in five movements

**2014–2019 · Build the rail.** Payment gateway, YC, Tiger Global, unicorn by 2020. One product,
one country.

**2020–2022 · Buy the platform.** COVID-era digital surge, $475M raised, $7.5bn peak. Seven
acquisitions turn a gateway into banking, payroll, POS, loyalty and lending. Revenue +76%, then +54%.

**2022–2023 · The regulator stops the music.** Onboarding banned for a year. **Revenue growth falls
to 0.7%.** Competitors take displaced merchants. Profit stays near zero.

**2024–2025 · Restructure for the exit.** Licence restored. Reverse flip completed at ~$150M tax
cost, producing a ₹1,209 cr accounting loss. Revenue rebounds +65%. Take rate expands 27%. TPV grows
only 20% — the monetisation pivot is visible in the numbers.

**2026 · Bet the company on AI.** Agentic payments (Feb), Agent Studio (Mar), Vulcan (Aug),
confidential DRHP (Jun), IPO targeted for year-end at a valuation ~27% below peak — and a hiring
programme for AI builders that is, in effect, this buildathon.

---

## 8. What this history means for your submission

**They are monetisation-hungry, not volume-hungry.** Revenue grew 3× faster than volume. Projects
that raise revenue *per merchant* fit the strategy; projects that just move more money do not.

**Regulatory standing is existential to them.** A year of lost onboarding cost them a year of growth.
Anything that looks like it complicates their regulatory position is dead on arrival — which is
precisely why the freeze-monitor framing mattered so much.

**They buy rather than build outside payments.** Payroll, loyalty, POS, fraud, lending tech were all
acquired. **A strong external agent is plausibly an acquisition or partnership target, not just a
competitor** — and Agent Studio already ships partner-built agents.

**The AI bet is six months old and unproven.** Vulcan launched days before this buildathon opened.
They are hiring builders because the bet needs people, and it needs to work before an IPO.
